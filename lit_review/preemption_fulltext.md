# Full-Text Pre-emption Verification (go/no-go)

Date: 2026-09-24. Method: full texts fetched and read. Six arXiv papers were downloaded as PDF and converted with `pdftotext -layout`; every number and quote below was checked against that raw text, not against a WebFetch summary (two WebFetch summaries were materially wrong on first pass: one claimed MTVA-Bench measures a "silent wrong" cell, one claimed τ-Elicitation isolates per-model noise effects; both corrected below). BFCL Audio has no arXiv id; OpenReview (id pgwHOpKkOp, ICML 2026 regular, Submission2330 camera-ready) gates its PDF behind a bot challenge, so the 19-page camera-ready PDF was obtained from the Lacuna mirror (`lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/pdf/art_d7a456be2df42016ecfd33cff7724a7a`) and read in full.

Our candidate, restated (MISSION.md §1): the SILENT-WRONG ledger for spoken-entity tool calls (schema-valid, executed, no error, no clarification asked, wrong semantics) by entity type × pipeline (P1 Whisper cascade, P2 audio-LLM transcriber → text LLM, P3 audio-native tool calling) × acoustic condition (voice/accent, rate, babble at fixed SNR, 8 kHz mu-law telephone band), WER as a measured covariate, and four recovery levers (none / read-back confirmation with a truthful oracle / ASR-confidence or n-best gating / ask-when-unsure instruction) scored on recovery fraction, extra turns, tokens, latency, and false-positive confirmations on already-correct calls. Pre-registered directions D1–D5.

Distance scale used in the verdict table: 0 = same measurement, 1 = same cell measured in a different pipeline or setting, 2 = names the cell and measures an adjacent quantity, 3 = adjacent instrument only.

## 1. τ-Elicitation (arXiv 2609.13602) — VERDICT: PARTIAL-HEAVY. Pre-empts the read-back-vs-instruction contrast and the asked/silent calibration for audio-native realtime agents; leaves the cascade side, confidence gating, telephone band, WER covariate and single-utterance command entities open.

**Citation:** Soham Ray (Sierra), Victor Barres (Mercor). "τ-Elicitation: Benchmarking Multi-Turn Entity Extraction in Voice Agents." arXiv:2609.13602v1 [cs.AI], 11 Sep 2026. 5 pages (ICASSP-style), code "available on GitHub".
**Full text:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13602 (read in full, 3,538 words).

**(a) Exact setup.**
- Agents: four audio-native realtime configurations only: "gpt-realtime-2 (minimal and xhigh reasoning), gemini-3.1-flash-live-preview (high), and grok-voice-think-fast-1.0 (provider default)" (§4). No cascade, no ASR anywhere on the agent side: "We intentionally omit caller-side ASR to avoid confounding agent performance with a second recognizer" (§6 Limitations). One "matched text run passes all 200 tasks, implicating spoken capture and repair rather than task understanding" (§5.3).
- Audio source: simulated caller is "gpt-5.5 (xhigh, temperature zero) with speech synthesized by ElevenLabs eleven v3" (§4). Five caller voices (Mildred, Wei, Priya, Mamadou, Arjun); the names suggest accent diversity but the paper gives NO accent labels, and it is one voice per name, so no "voices per accent" exists.
- Acoustic conditions: three environment realizations: "regular; noise-heavy, with 10 dB background noise, bursts, frame drops, and muffling; and speech-heavy, with interruptions, backchannels, and restarts" (§3.3). No telephone band, no speaking-rate manipulation, no SNR sweep. Six "caller realisms" (spelling style "zed", self-correction, spelling variation, falter-and-restart spelling, partial/wrong-field answers, mispronunciation) are caller-behaviour manipulations, not acoustic ones.
- Entity types: 10 banks × 80 values (40 easy, 40 hard): dates, phones, times, person names, codes (VINs, mod-97 IDs), properties, addresses, emails, coined names, medications (Table 2). No AMOUNTS and no QUANTITIES.
- Task count: 200 core single-entity tasks (10 easy + 10 hard per bank) plus linked two/three-field tasks; 600 agent-directed + 600 scaffolded calls per configuration = 4,800 calls. Task shape is ELICITATION: "Each task is a callback about a record with missing fields. The agent sees the field names, asks the caller for their values, logs its captures, and submits once; the caller reveals a value only after it is requested" (§3.1). The tool is a single database write. The caller "supplies requested values, spells accurately when asked, and confirms or corrects read-backs, but never volunteers information."

**(b) SILENT-WRONG cell / asked-vs-silent split.** PARTIALLY. Scoring is exact string match against gold: "We compare each final database value with the exact gold string" (§3.3); no schema validation step, no distinction between a schema-valid wrong value and a malformed one (all their values are free strings written to a DB, so every wrong submission is by construction "silent" at the executor). They do NOT name a silent-wrong cell. But §5.2 gives the capture-level 2×2 that is our Core Question 4: "Initial captures are wrong for 69% of GPT xhigh fields, 65% of Gemini fields, and 42% of Grok fields. GPT and Gemini verify 66% and 64% of wrong captures versus 50% of correct ones; Grok verifies nearly everything (94%/96%)." From these, unverified-wrong (our silent-wrong at the capture level) is derivable (roughly a third of wrong GPT/Gemini captures go unverified), and verification-of-correct (our false-positive confirmation cost) is 50% for GPT/Gemini and 96% for Grok. Repair after asking is separately measured: "Only 27%, 37%, and 24% of verified GPT xhigh, Gemini, and Grok errors end correct. Among 347 initially wrong captures, recovery is 10% without a repair step, 26% with read-back only, 24% with spelling only, and 35% with both."

**(c) Pipeline comparison.** NOT COVERED. Audio-native only; no Whisper, no transcriber arm, no cascade. Cannot cross pipeline with acoustic condition.

**(d) Recovery levers.** TWO ARMS, both prompt-level, on audio-native agents only:
- Agent-directed (default prompt) = our lever (d) "ask when unsure" instruction: "the record must be exactly correct and letter-by-letter verification is suggested when unsure, but the agent decides when and how to verify" (§1).
- Scaffolded = our lever (b) read-back confirmation (plus spelling): "prescribes asking, spelling or reading back, confirming, correcting, and then submitting."
- Result: "raises Pass@1 by 15–37 points and Pass³ by 14–31 points (Figure 2), while adding 21–28 seconds per call. Gains are slightly larger for hard entities (27 versus 23 points)" (§5.1). Regular-condition Pass@1: easy 64.0% → 87.3%, hard 44.3% → 71.3% (§5.3). Multi-field: "Validate each field; retry errors" raises task Pass@1 from .64 to .82 (Table 3), "we do not attribute the gain to either one alone."
- Costs reported: seconds per call (21–28 s), verification-effort counts (Fig. 3, read-backs + spelling requests per call, 0–3), mispronunciation realism "adds 24 points of spelling requests and 26 seconds." NOT reported: tokens, extra turns as a count, false-positive confirmations as a lever cost (the 50%-of-correct-captures-verified figure is reported as agent behaviour, not charged against the scaffold, which by design verifies everything).
- No ASR-confidence / n-best gating (impossible in their design: no ASR).

**(e) WER as covariate.** NO. No WER, no transcript metric, by explicit design (Limitations quote above). A "speech-fidelity judge" scores the AGENT's TTS output (severity ≥ 2 defect share 2.1–4.1%, Table 4), which "do not yet affect task reward."

**(f) Per-entity / per-voice breakdown.** YES on both, audio-native only. Per entity (Table 2, pooled agent-directed Pass³): Dates .60, Phones .47, Times .43, Person names .30, Codes .25, Properties .18, Addresses .10, Emails .08, Coined .03, Medications .02. Authors' reading: "format-constrained versus lexical capture. Dates and phone numbers follow tightly scoped numeric schemas." Per voice: "the gap between each system's best- and worst-performing voices is 27 points for GPT xhigh, 28 for Gemini, and 15 for Grok. The ordering is not stable: Mildred leads GPT xhigh and Grok, whereas Wei leads Gemini... Mildred significantly exceeds Priya, Mamadou, and Arjun, but not Wei. Yet no system increases verification for its own lowest-performing voice." Per environment: only pooled Pass³ and "only Grok increases effort under noise" (Fig. 3); no per-condition Pass@1 by model is printed.

**(g) Limitations / future work, verbatim.** "We study English, three providers, one simulator, and one run per main environment. We intentionally omit caller-side ASR to avoid confounding agent performance with a second recognizer. As a result, agent speech synthesis is not evaluated end to end: the speech-fidelity judge reports synthesis defects, but they do not yet affect task reward. The multi-field study is formative; broader provider, human-caller, and multilingual validation remain future work." Positioning claim (§2): "None combines live tool execution, exact database writes, controlled entity type, difficulty and count, an agent's choice to verify."

**(h) Verdict for our candidate.**
- Core Q3 (read-back vs instruction): PRE-EMPTED for audio-native realtime agents in an elicitation loop. The two arms are exactly our (b) and (d), the oracle caller is exactly our truthful oracle, and the number (+14–31 Pass³, 21–28 s) is published. What remains: (c) confidence gating (needs a cascade), token/turn costs, and the false-positive bill charged to the lever.
- Core Q4 (calibration): PRE-EMPTED at the capture level for audio-native (66/64/94% of wrong verified vs 50/50/96% of correct). Remains open for cascades and for single-utterance commands.
- Core Q1 (silent-wrong by entity × pipeline): PARTIAL. Per-entity failure rates exist (audio-native only, elicitation only, exact-string not schema-valid framing). No pipeline axis.
- Core Q2 (acoustic × WER): LEFT OPEN. Noise is one pooled environment; no telephone band, no rate, no SNR sweep, no WER by design.
- D1: PARTIALLY PRE-EMPTED WITH OPPOSITE SIGN on the digit half. They find phones the second-EASIEST bank (.47) and dates the easiest (.60); our D1 predicts digit strings among the WORST. Their caller spells and confirms on request, which favours digit strings; in our single-utterance command regime (no spelling available before the call) the sign may differ, but we must pre-register against their finding and cite it. Amounts are untested by them.
- D2: LEFT OPEN (no cascade). D3: the read-back > instruction half is pre-empted for audio-native; the confidence-gating comparison is open. D4: LEFT OPEN. D5: PARTIALLY pre-empted (per-voice spread of 15–28 points is published; the within-accent vs between-accent decomposition is not, since no accents are labelled and there is one voice per name).
- Cite as the direct predecessor and delineate on four axes: (i) elicitation with a spelling-capable caller vs single-utterance commands with embedded entities; (ii) audio-native only vs three pipelines on identical clips; (iii) no ASR/WER vs WER as a per-cell covariate; (iv) prompt-level levers only vs a signal-level lever (confidence/n-best gating) with the false-positive bill.

## 2. From Text to Voice (arXiv 2605.15104) — VERDICT: PARTIAL. Has cascade vs direct-voice on the same tasks, but clean audio only, one ASR, argument-value error as a share of failures, no clarification cell, no lever.

**Citation:** Md Tahmid Rahman Laskar, Xue-Yong Fu, Seyyed Saeed Sarfjoo, Quinten McNamara, Jonas Robertson, Shashi Bhushan TN (Dialpad). "From Text to Voice: A Reproducible and Verifiable Framework for Evaluating Tool Calling LLM Agents." arXiv:2605.15104v2 [cs.CL], 20 May 2026. 20 pages. Code: github.com/talkiq/dialpad-ai-research/tree/main/toolvoice.
**Full text:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15104 (read in full).

**(a) Exact setup.** Seven omni-modal models on audio: GPT-4o-Realtime, GPT-Realtime-1.5, GPT-Realtime-Mini, Gemini-2.5-Flash-Live, Gemini-3.1-Flash-Live, Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct, Phi-4-Multimodal. Cascade (§4.3.3, Table 4): "For transcription, we use the GPT-4o-Transcribe model", feeding the same three realtime models in text mode (Gemini-3.1-Flash-Live, GPT-Realtime-1.5, Qwen3-Omni). No Whisper in the cascade (Whisper large-v3 is used only to compute TTS intelligibility WER, Table 1). Audio: TTS only, three engines (Gemini-2.5-Flash-TTS, Gemini-2.5-Pro-TTS, GPT-4o-Mini-TTS) × two voices each (Kore/Orus, Coral/Ash) = six configurations; no accent variation. Noise: DEMAND environmental noise at 5/10/15/20 dB SNR plus clean; no telephone band, no rate. Tasks: Confetti (313 instances, tool call required) and When2Call (300 instances, call-vs-no-call decision). Argument types: whatever Confetti's schemas contain; not stratified.

**(b) SILENT-WRONG cell.** PARTIAL. Table 5 (paired text-correct/audio-wrong failures) defines "argument-value errors, where it selects the correct tool and argument fields but fills one or more fields with incorrect, incomplete, or misheard content": 57.2% of Gemini-3.1-Flash-Live failures, 54.3% GPT-Realtime-1.5, 39.5% Qwen3-Omni. This is a share of failures, not a rate per task, and it is not crossed with whether a clarification was asked. Clarification appears only as a When2Call outcome class (Fig. 3 confusion matrices), not as a silent-vs-asked split on entity calls. Scoring is AST soft match (exact for non-strings, AlignScore for strings), never executed.

**(c) Pipeline comparison.** YES but narrow: Table 4, Confetti, CLEAN audio only. Clean text / direct voice / cascade: Gemini-3.1-Flash-Live 73.0 / 70.4 / 71.3; GPT-Realtime-1.5 64.0 / 59.2 / 58.8; Qwen3-Omni 62.2 / 60.4 / 58.9. "neither architecture uniformly dominates." Noise robustness (Fig. 2) is evaluated for Qwen3-Omni direct only: "moderate environmental noise is not the primary source of performance loss for this model." Pipeline × noise: NOT crossed.

**(d) Recovery levers.** NONE. Grep of full text: no confirmation, read-back, confidence gating or clarification prompting experiment.

**(e) WER as covariate.** WER is reported for the TTS audio (Table 1: 3.4–4.8% clean, ~7.3% at 20 dB) as an intelligibility check, never related to argument error.

**(f) Breakdowns.** Per voice: Tables 2–3 give per-TTS-configuration accuracies (range ~4 points for Gemini-3.1). No per-argument-type, no accent.

**(g) Limitations / future work, verbatim (§5).** "We evaluate two datasets and a finite set of current models; broader domains and additional text benchmarks such as BFCL..., τ-bench..., as well as MCP benchmarks... remain important extensions. Our audio is TTS-generated, so it should be viewed as a controlled first-stage proxy rather than a replacement for spontaneous calls with natural conversational artifacts. Future work should extend the framework to naturally spoken customer-support data..., richer stress-test suites, alongside cost–latency evaluation."

**(h) Verdict.** Core Q1: PARTIAL (argument-value errors dominate audio failures; no silent/asked split; no entity typing; no P2 transcriber arm). Core Q2: LEFT OPEN for pipelines (noise only on one direct model). Core Q3/Q4: LEFT OPEN. D2: PARTIAL evidence that cascade ≈ direct on clean audio; no clarification-rate axis. D4: OPEN. Cite for the "same tasks, cascade vs direct" design; delineate: clean-only, one ASR, no lever, no silent/asked cell.

## 3. SpeechGym (arXiv 2608.26432) — VERDICT: PARTIAL-LIGHT. Quantifies mis-heard slot values (32% of speech rollouts vs 2% text) in an RL gym, but the errors SURFACE as tool errors, no pipeline axis, no lever ablation.

**Citation:** Jiajun Fan, Jingyuan Li, Prashanth Gurunath Shivakumar, Jia-Hong Huang, Qi Luo, M. Maruf, Ivan Bulyko, Ge Liu, Roger Ren (UIUC / Amazon AGI). "SpeechGym: An Audio-Native Gym for Training Voice Agents via Reinforcement Learning." arXiv:2608.26432, 26 Aug 2026. 14 pages.
**Full text:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.26432 (read in full).

**(a) Exact setup.** "two omni-modal models converse in native audio, with no external ASR or TTS and no API boundary": agent = Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B (LoRA on the Thinker), user simulator = frozen Qwen3-Omni. Audio is the user model's own speech output; no external TTS, no voice variation, clean self-play audio in training. Domains: τ²-bench Airline, Retail, Telecom "plus Banking, added for long, high-stakes numeric slots." Slot types named in prose: "a digit of a zip code, a character of an order ID, a spelling of a name"; account numbers, transaction amounts in Banking. Out-of-environment evaluation on τ-Voice, whose audio "comes from different TTS voices over a narrowband, telephony-grade channel" (§4.4), i.e. telephone band exists but is not a manipulated factor. Task count not stated (τ²-bench tasks).

**(b) SILENT-WRONG cell.** NO, and importantly their entity errors are NOT silent. Table 1: "Mis-hearing (slot value) 32% / 2% / 16×; Tool error rate 42% / 26% / 1.6×; Dead loop 29% / 18%." §4.2: "The agent picks the right tool and the right argument slot, then fills it with a mis-heard value... The database check is exact, so one confused character is worth the same as no attempt." The causal chain they describe is "a mis-hearing produces a wrong argument, the wrong argument a tool error, the tool error a retry" — the mis-heard value is a lookup key that fails against the database, so the failure is ERROR-SURFACED in our taxonomy. Their "Confidently-wrong over-action" is about unauthorised writes under caller pressure, not entity corruption. No asked-vs-silent split.

**(c) Pipeline comparison.** NO. Audio-native self-play vs text channel of the same model; τ-Voice's user side is a commercial cascade but the agent is always the omni model. Not crossed with noise.

**(d) Recovery levers.** Emergent, not tested: "asking the caller to spell a name out, retrying a lookup with a corrected spelling... Nothing in the reward mentions spelling." Post-training: "Recovers after a mis-hearing, higher better: 42% → 62% (+20)" (Table 3). No confirmation/gating ablation, no lever cost.

**(e) WER.** NOT reported.

**(f) Breakdowns.** None by slot type or voice (single user model).

**(g) Limitations / future work.** No formal section. Broader Impact: "More capable voice agents nevertheless carry deployment risks a simulated gym does not address, and responsible deployment requires safeguards outside its scope: content filtering, explicit user consent for recorded or synthesised speech, and reliable escalation to a human." Conclusion frames the text-to-audio gap as "an optimisation problem with an objective, a gradient and a stopping criterion."

**(h) Verdict.** Core Q1: PARTIAL as motivation only (the 16× mis-hearing figure is the number a reviewer will quote; but in their domains wrong keys error out, so the silent cell is untouched). Q2–Q4: OPEN. D1–D5: OPEN. Cite for the 32%/2% amplification and for the observation that the plan is right and only the value is wrong; delineate: lookup-key entities that surface as tool errors vs schema-valid entities (amounts, dates, quantities) that execute.

## 4. VoiceCodeBench (arXiv 2608.28916) — VERDICT: PARTIAL on the WER-decoupling instrument; ASR-only, no LLM, no tool call, no noise, no lever.

**Citation:** Tyler Baumgartner, Brandon Tai, Lisa Kaelin-Martin, Candice Fan, Luc Debaupte, Bill Wang, Yi Zhong (Besimple AI). "VoiceCodeBench: Evaluating Exact Structured-Token Recovery in Automatic Speech Recognition." arXiv:2608.28916v2, Sep 2026. 7 pages.
**Full text:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.28916 (read in full).

**(a) Exact setup.** ASR systems only: 19 batch/streaming configurations (Whisper large-v3, Deepgram Nova-3, gpt-transcribe, gpt-live-transcribe, ElevenLabs Scribe v2, AssemblyAI Universal-3 Pro, Google Chirp 3, Meta Muse, Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3, Cartesia Ink 2, Inworld STT 1, Inkling-NVFP4). A GPT-5.5 verifier scores entity recoverability from the transcript. Audio: HUMAN recordings, 300 segments, 5.59 h, 85 speakers, "deliberate workplace dictation", no noise or channel manipulation. Entities: 1,482 targets in 26 types / 6 families (contact/routing, network/web, code/system, identifiers, numeric/measurement, language form). "raw-audio-only protocol": no prompts, vocab, or post-ASR correction.

**(b) SILENT-WRONG cell.** NO. "Executed downstream actions fall outside the evaluation." No LLM, no call, no clarification.

**(c) Pipelines.** NO.

**(d) Levers.** NONE tested; named as deployment options: "Applications may improve on them with candidate lists, domain context, confirmation turns, typed parsers, range checks, or human review, then report those conditions separately."

**(e) WER as covariate.** YES, system-level only: "Across the 19 systems, Spearman correlation is ρ = −0.28 between WER and CTEM and ρ = −0.22 between WER and TSR." Without the outlier ρ = −0.15 / −0.08. "AssemblyAI Universal-3 Pro batch has the lowest WER but ranks twelfth on CTEM." They caveat: "They do not measure recording-level associations or establish that changing WER causes a change in CTEM." (v2 replaced two −0.73 values from v1.)

**(f) Breakdowns.** Per entity family (Table 7 CTEM): Numeric/measurement 93.7%, Language form 92.1%, Identifiers 87.3%, Contact/routing 75.3%, Code/system 69.1%, Network/web 65.9%. Hardest types (Table 6 median): URL 46.8%, command 52.0%, file path 54.9%, postal address 55.0%, email 63.1%. Per speaker: "The 85-speaker sample is insufficient for fine-grained subgroup claims."

**(g) Limitations / future work, verbatim (§5.2).** "VoiceCodeBench is English-only and uses deliberate workplace dictation with synthetic scenarios. It does not represent all spontaneous, overlapping, noisy, long-form, multilingual, or domain-specific speech. The 85-speaker sample is insufficient for fine-grained subgroup claims... Several of the 19 configurations share providers or model families, creating dependence within the ASR sample. The rank correlations are descriptive... Future work should add languages, spontaneous speech, channel noise, and application context."

**(h) Verdict.** D4's WER-decoupling half: PARTIALLY PRE-EMPTED at the system-ranking level for ASR alone (the qualitative claim "WER does not predict entity recovery" is published). OPEN: within-pipeline, across acoustic conditions, at the executed-call level. D1: their numeric family is the EASIEST (93.7%), again against our digit-string prediction, but dictation with human speakers and no noise. Cite for the instrument; delineate: transcript recoverability vs executed argument correctness, no noise/channel axis.

## 5. MTVA-Bench (arXiv 2609.20152) — VERDICT: PARTIAL-LIGHT. Names our exact failure in its opening paragraph, then builds an instrument that deliberately steers ASR damage AWAY from the entity values. No audio at all.

**Citation:** Pritish Mishra, Ishaan Kumar, Akshat Mandoli, Sudarshan Kamath (Smallest AI). "MTVA-Bench: Evaluating the Language Model Inside Cascaded Voice Agents." arXiv:2609.20152v1, 17 Sep 2026. 11 pages.
**Full text:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20152 (read in full).

**(a) Exact setup.** Text-only simulation of the LM slot in a cascade: "holds the model's production interface fixed and simulating everything on the other side." No ASR, no TTS, no audio: "The benchmark measures nothing about sound." Channel damage is authored text edits: "An authored ASR event drops, doubles, or substitutes a specific span at a specific turn. A lighter seeded mode changes one eligible word of the first caller turn, drawn from words of four letters or more and never from the caller's known values, which keeps the damage away from the exact fact the call depends on unless a scenario puts it there" (§4.2). Only "53 [scenarios] author channel damage: 10 light ASR, 43 natural VAD splits" (Table 2). Models: gpt-5.6-sol, electron, gpt-5.2, phonellm, gemini-3.6-flash, gpt-5.6-luna, qwen3.8-27b. 490 scenarios, 49 authored agents, 371 tools, 9 domains, 9 languages. Mock backend: "answers each call based on the arguments the model actually sent. A wrong argument produces a failure response instead of the happy path."

**(b) SILENT-WRONG cell.** NO (correcting the first WebFetch summary). The paper's word "silent" means the model emitted a tool call with no spoken text around it: "counting a call as silent when its message carries no spoken text and no concluding message. gpt-5.2 is silent on 2,672 of its 2,765 tool calls" (§7). That is a conversational-conduct diagnostic, unrelated to wrong-argument execution. The motivating example IS ours: "the recognizer garbles one digit of the tracking number. The model behind the agent picks the right API, reschedule_delivery, so a function-calling benchmark would count this call as a success. It also passes the garbled number" (§1). But the mock backend converts a wrong argument into a failure response, and the seeded damage avoids known values, so the schema-valid-executed-wrong cell is never populated except by hand in ≤10 scenarios. "Channel repair" diagnostic asks "Did the model recover a declared garbled word or join declared fragments?" with no rate table.

**(c) Pipelines.** NO (cascade LM only, no audio-native, no acoustic axis).

**(d) Levers.** NONE.

**(e) WER.** NOT reported; damage is authored per span.

**(f) Breakdowns.** Per language and difficulty (Table 6); no entity type, no voice.

**(g) Limitations / future work, verbatim (§9–10).** "No audio. The benchmark measures nothing about sound. Recognition error rates, TTS quality, pronunciation, accent, emotion in the voice, background noise, overlapping speech, and latency are all out of scope, and text-level damage stands in for only a narrow slice of what real pipelines do." ... "Corpus coverage... Only 53 scenarios author channel damage." ... "And the corpus needs many more scenarios with authored recognition damage, since the current 53 are too few to support claims about it."

**(h) Verdict.** Everything OPEN. Cite for the motivating example and the "wrong argument → failure response" design choice; delineate: real audio through real recognizers with schema-valid wrong values that DO execute, which is the cell their backend and their damage policy both avoid. Their own future-work line ("many more scenarios with authored recognition damage") is where our gap sits.

## 6. Proactive for Uncertainty (arXiv 2605.25404) — VERDICT: PARTIAL on the confidence-gating baseline; transcript-level error detection in a cascade, no tool calls, no entities, no read-back arm.

**Citation:** Yizhou Peng, Ziyang Ma, Changsong Liu, Yi-Wen Chao, Xie Chen, Eng Siong Chng. "Proactive for Uncertainty: Cause-Aware Error Diagnosis and Interactive Clarification for Spoken Dialogue Systems." arXiv:2605.25404v1, 25 May 2026. 19 pages.
**Full text:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.25404 (read in full).

**(a) Exact setup.** Cascade: Parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2 ASR with four learned detector heads (deletion, perception, comprehension, distortion-event) on the frozen encoder; GPT-5.2 as dialogue manager and user simulator; CosyVoice3 TTS for the simulated user's replies. Audio: real corpora (LibriSpeech, SPGISpeech2 financial teleconference, AESRC2020 eight accents: American, British, Chinese, Indian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, Russian; OOD Gigaspeech, WSJ, OpenHermes, Alpaca read by TTS) with synthetic distortions: additive noise SNR [−5, 20] dB, interference SNR [5, 20] dB, RIR, Opus packet loss 1–8 kbps, temporal masking. No 8 kHz mu-law band, no rate. Task: detect ASR errors by cause and run up to three clarification rounds; NO tool/function calling, NO entity slots; the outcome is WER reduction and a Model-as-Judge dialogue-quality score.

**(b) SILENT-WRONG cell / calibration.** NO executed-call cell. The nearest object is word-level detection Recall vs False-Positive Rate (Table 2), which is a token-level version of our asked/silent × right/wrong matrix, but on transcripts, not on calls.

**(c) Pipelines.** Cascade only, argued as the industrial default ("explainability and rapid debugging are paramount"); end-to-end models mentioned, not evaluated.

**(d) Levers.** Confidence gating IS the baseline: Tsallis-entropy word confidence with a threshold "tuned to match the FPR of the proposed method." Word-level, SPGI2-Test comprehension: baseline recall 23.66% at FPR 4.01% vs proposed 57.96% at 3.98%; perception average 41.57% vs 52.26% at ~9.65% FPR (Table 2). Authors: the entropy baseline fails on "confidently wrong" transcripts in clean audio. Costs: FPR is reported (the false-alarm bill); turns are fixed at ≤3 rounds; no latency/token cost; downstream MaJ dialogue quality 74.6 → 83.0 (OpenHermes) after 3 rounds vs 85.4 clean ceiling (Table 5). No read-back arm, no instruction-only arm, no n-best disagreement.

**(e) WER.** WER is the primary outcome (4.23–26.25% before, 3.85–12.28% after clarification; WERR up to 29.9%), not a covariate against entity error.

**(f) Breakdowns.** Per accent WER (Table 9: American 3.74, British 0.72, Indian 6.08, Japanese 9.74, Russian 7.81) and per distortion; no entity, no per-speaker.

**(g) Limitations, verbatim.** "We deliberately constrained the LLM to edit only detector-flagged tokens to isolate detector precision, which may cause the system to miss unflagged errors and underestimate LLM–detector synergy. All experiments target English speech only... Training data is drawn from SPGISpeech2 (financial) and AESRC (Accented English) with simulated distortions, so performance may degrade under real-world conditions (e.g., non-stationary noise, overlapping speakers) or unseen domains. We report automatic metrics without subjective human validation, leaving open the question of whether cause-aware clarifications feel natural to users. Finally, artifacts from the zero-shot voice-cloning TTS can bias end-to-end scores downward, making our results a conservative lower bound." No future-work section.

**(h) Verdict.** Core Q3 lever (c): PARTIAL (a confidence-gated clarification baseline with matched-FPR reporting exists, on transcripts). OPEN: gating applied to entity arguments of tool calls, compared head-to-head with read-back and instruction-only, with the false-positive bill charged as confirmations on already-correct CALLS. Cite as the methodological template for "gate at matched FPR"; delineate: transcript repair vs executed-call recovery.

## 7. BFCL Audio (ICML 2026, OpenReview pgwHOpKkOp; no arXiv id) — VERDICT: PARTIAL-HEAVY. Has both suites (pipelined via three ASRs, end-to-end), controlled noise, AST argument grading, and a taxonomy naming our cell ("valid but incorrect function call", "the model never asks for confirmation"). Does NOT report per-entity-type or per-condition silent-wrong RATES, has no telephone band, uses WER only as a sanity check, and tests no recovery lever.

**Citation:** Huanzhi Mao, Aditya Ghai, Imra Dawoodani, Tony A. Ginart, Shishir G. Patil, John Emmons, Joseph E. Gonzalez (UC Berkeley; Salesforce AI Research). "BFCL Audio: An Audio Function Calling Evaluation for Large Language Models." Proceedings of the 43rd ICML, Seoul, PMLR 306, 2026. 19 pages camera-ready. (Running header in the PDF reads "An Audio Function Calling Benchmark"; the ICML poster page lists the title with an "MFCL" typo.)
**Full text:** OpenReview PDF `/pdf/6f490c16230172862f231101f5b7e8cd56867f06.pdf` (challenge-gated); read from the Lacuna mirror listed above. Related: Salesforce blog `salesforce.com/blog/bfcl-audio-benchmark/`; code in `github.com/ShishirPatil/gorilla`.

**(a) Exact setup.**
- Two suites: "BFCL Text Audio (pipelined ASR → LLM → tools via transcripts) and BFCL True Audio (end-to-end audio-in → tool calls)." Text Audio transcribes each utterance with THREE ASR engines: "Deepgram nova-2 (English), OpenAI gpt-4o-transcribe, and ElevenLabs scribe_v1... the three outputs are used independently throughout evaluation to expose provider-level variability" (App. F). No Whisper.
- Models (Table 2): E2E on clean and noisy audio: GPT-4o-audio, GPT-4o-mini-audio, Qwen3-Omni-Flash, Gemini-2.5-Flash, Gemini-2.5-Pro. Text-suite models: the same five in text mode plus GLM-4.5, Claude-Opus-4.1, Grok-4-0709, Qwen3-235B-A22B, xLAM-2-70b, GPT-5.
- Audio: "approximately 225 distinct voice clones" via "ElevenLabs Professional Voice Cloning (PVC) with the eleven_monolingual_v1 model" from Mozilla Common Voice speakers, "vary in speaker identity, accent, prosody"; the abstract's "accent and speaking-rate variation" lives inside the clone pool; there is NO accent or rate factor in any result table.
- Acoustic conditions (§3.1.4): MUSAN/CHiME-5 background "mixed at random signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs)" (no fixed SNR reported), competing speech at varied SNRs, network impairments (10–40 ms frame drops, micro-loops, tail truncation), device/room effects (RIR, clipping, gain shifts, mic rubbing). No 8 kHz mu-law / telephone band.
- Tasks: 6.2K, derived from BFCL v4 single-turn (Simple / Multiple / Parallel / Parallel-Multiple, expert-curated and community) and multi-turn (Base / Miss Func / Miss Param), plus Relevance/Irrelevance. Argument types are BFCL's (IDs, dates, cities, file paths...), not stratified into entity types. Disfluency injection uses 23–26 speech features including "numbers_noise" and explicit spelling ("j-o-h-n at gmail dot com").
- Grading: AST match without execution; "values are compared with type-aware normalization"; multi-turn by state + minimal-path check. Clarification: "we allow whitelisted confirmation turns... An LLM judge validates clarification requests; if valid, a simulated user provides a brief, whitelisted reply (e.g., spelling of proper nouns)... Clarification turns are permitted but not scored" (§4).

**(b) SILENT-WRONG cell / asked-vs-silent split.** NAMED, NOT RATED. Abstract/§1: speech "can silently alter entities and slot values." §5.1: "Under noise, the dominant error source shifts from detecting a request to transcribing its details, producing subtle yet harmful semantic errors: the assistant executes an action, but the wrong one." FM3 Parameter Distortion: "'fifty' becomes 'fifteen,' yielding a valid but incorrect function call." FM6 Named-Entity Errors: "the model never asks for confirmation, instead generating a faulty call (Fig. 3). Noisy audio worsens this across all architectures, but is most acute for noisy E2E runs." FM4 Clarification Misfires: "irrelevant clarification questions that are not about named entities, especially in pipelined models with noisy ASR... End-to-end (E2E) models perform better due to lower uncertainty." Fig. 3 gives failure-mode SHARES per model; there is no per-task rate of "executed-wrong-without-asking", no 2×2 against correctness, and a valid confirmation on an already-correct value is answered and never counted, so the false-positive confirmation cost is invisible. Impact Statement: "reduce user harm caused by silent transcription or argument errors when tools are executed."

**(c) Pipeline comparison.** YES, partially crossed. Table 2 has the same model in three roles for GPT-4o-audio (Text 58.6 / Clean Audio 60.4 / Noisy Audio 50.9), Qwen3-Omni-Flash (55.1 / 55.8 / 48.2), Gemini-2.5-Flash (— / 53.0 / 45.6), Gemini-2.5-Pro (51.5 / 51.1 / 45.4), GPT-4o-mini-audio (— / 47.2 / 40.3). Caption: "End-to-end models on clean audio typically surpass pipelined text-only systems by leveraging contextual and prosodic cues, but their advantage is brittle: noise induces sharp degradation, often below the steadier pipelined baselines." However the "Text" rows are NOT split by clean vs noisy transcript or by ASR engine in Table 2, so the pipeline × noise cross is asserted in the caption, not tabulated for the pipelined arm. §6.1 shows disfluency ablations per suite (2,520 instances) and App. C Fig. 6 stratifies pipelined failures by ASR provider. No audio-LLM-as-transcriber arm (our P2).

**(d) Recovery levers.** NONE tested as a lever. Every Text Audio run carries an ask-when-unsure system prompt (App. E: "Do not trust the transcription text blindly, especially on important information... If the text appears garbled, double-check with the user instead of guessing"), so the instruction is a constant, not an arm. Whitelisted clarification is a protocol allowance with an oracle reply, not a measured recovery. No read-back, no confidence gating, no cost (turns/seconds/tokens). Conclusion names the gap: "reliable speech agents require robustness beyond text function calling, including uncertainty-aware confirmation strategies."

**(e) WER as covariate.** DECLINED BY DESIGN, with a qualitative decoupling claim. Table 3 caption: "WER is included as a supplementary sanity check only, as it does not reliably track tool-use failures; mic rubbing, for example, shows near-zero intent loss despite non-trivial WER, whereas competing speech causes severe intent loss at comparable WER." §6.2: "Device artifacts are mostly benign... illustrating why WER alone can be misleading for tool use." No WER numbers appear in the printed Table 3 columns (Intent Pres., Confidence, Samples). Noise results are transcript-level intent preservation (competing speech 42.4%, reverb echo 48.8%, network cuts 50.6%, background noise 74.6%, device artifacts ≥96%; Table 3; 6,480 noisy variants from 243 clean utterances), not executed-call outcomes.

**(f) Breakdowns.** Per entity type: NO (only intent categories Navigation/Dictation/Command/Question under competing speech, Table 4; and "filepath verbalization" as the dominant E2E disfluency failure). Per voice/accent: NO, despite 225 clones. Per ASR provider: yes for disfluency failures (App. C Fig. 6).

**(g) Limitations / future work, verbatim (§7).** "BFCL Audio is designed to approximate deployment-relevant speech and acoustic conditions in a controlled and reproducible way; nevertheless, it remains a synthetic benchmark. Voice-cloned speech, rewritten transcripts, and simulated acoustic perturbations cannot fully capture the spontaneity, recording variability, and environmental complexity of naturally occurring interactions. Benchmarks built from real recordings, manual transcripts, and authentic deployment settings, such as office environments with naturally occurring background noise, would provide stronger ecological validity. We therefore view BFCL Audio as a controlled diagnostic benchmark that complements, rather than replaces, evaluation on fully real-world data, and we consider the construction of such naturalistic corpora an important direction for future work." Impact Statement adds: "model performance may remain uneven across accents and acoustic environments."

**(h) Verdict.** Core Q1: PARTIAL-HEAVY. The cell is named in their vocabulary (FM3/FM6, "valid but incorrect", "never asks for confirmation") and the two-suite design exists; the RATE by entity type × pipeline × condition does not, and grading is AST (unexecuted) with no silent/asked split. Core Q2: PARTIAL (noise types ranked by intent preservation; no fixed SNR, no telephone band, no rate factor, no per-accent result, WER explicitly not used). Core Q3: OPEN (no lever; instruction is a constant). Core Q4: OPEN as a rate (clarification misfires are a failure share; valid confirmations are unscored). D2: QUALITATIVELY ANTICIPATED in our direction ("pipelined models with noisy ASR" over-ask; "E2E... never asks for confirmation... most acute for noisy E2E runs") but unquantified; ours would be the number. D4: the "WER misleads" claim is PARTIALLY PRE-EMPTED qualitatively; the quantitative decoupling of executed silent-wrong from WER across conditions within a pipeline is open. D1, D3, D5: OPEN. This is the paper to delineate against most carefully alongside τ-Elicitation: they built the benchmark; we would measure the ledger and the lever on it (or on a compatible smaller bank).

## What remains ours, and the reframing this forces

**Claims we can no longer make first.**
1. "Read-back confirmation recovers most spoken-entity errors in voice agents at a cost of one extra turn / ~25 s." τ-Elicitation published this for four audio-native realtime agents (+14–31 Pass³, 21–28 s) with a truthful oracle caller and a spelling-capable protocol.
2. "Agents ask more when wrong but still miss a large share; they verify correct values needlessly." τ-Elicitation's 66/64/94% vs 50/50/96% is that calibration for audio-native agents.
3. "Argument-value corruption is the dominant audio-induced tool-calling failure and WER does not track it." From Text to Voice (Table 5), BFCL Audio (FM3/FM6, Table 3 caption) and VoiceCodeBench (ρ = −0.28) each carry a version of this.
4. "A two-suite cascade-vs-native benchmark under noise." BFCL Audio, ICML 2026.
5. D1 as written is contradicted on the digit half by two papers (phones second-easiest in τ-Elicitation; numeric easiest in VoiceCodeBench). It must be re-pre-registered as a test of whether that ordering survives when the caller cannot spell (single-utterance commands) and when the entity is an amount (untested anywhere).

**Claims that remain ours (verified against all seven full texts).**
1. The SILENT-WRONG RATE as a per-task quantity with the four-way ledger (correct / silent-wrong / error-surfaced / asked), on executed mock tools with schema validation, by entity type. Nobody rates it: τ-E scores exact strings with no schema step and no error-surfaced class; BFCL Audio grades ASTs unexecuted and reports failure shares; SpeechGym's wrong keys error out; MTVA's backend converts wrong arguments to failures.
2. The same clips through three pipelines including the audio-LLM-as-transcriber arm (P2), with silent-wrong and ask-rate per pipeline. BFCL Audio has pipelined vs E2E but no P2 and no per-condition split of the pipelined arm; From Text to Voice is clean-only with one ASR.
3. Telephone band (8 kHz mu-law) crossed with pipeline. Absent from all seven (SpeechGym's τ-Voice channel is narrowband but constant).
4. WER measured per cell and plotted against silent-wrong across conditions within a pipeline (the decoupling curve). BFCL Audio explicitly refuses to use WER; τ-E has none; VoiceCodeBench is system-level ASR-only.
5. Confidence / n-best gating as a lever on tool-call arguments, head-to-head with read-back and instruction-only, with recovery fraction AND the false-positive bill (confirmations spent on already-correct calls, extra turns, tokens). Proactive gives the matched-FPR template on transcripts; τ-E cannot gate (no ASR); BFCL Audio tests no lever.
6. Amounts and quantities as entity types in the silent-wrong regime (τ-E: no amounts; BFCL Audio: "fifty → fifteen" as an anecdote only).
7. The single-utterance command regime ("send fifty dollars to Priya") where the entity arrives embedded and the agent must decide to read back BEFORE executing, versus τ-E's elicitation loop where the caller can be asked to spell. This is the deployment shape of payments/booking commands and it changes what "ask" costs.
8. Multiple voices PER ACCENT with two TTS families, so the within-accent vs between-accent decomposition (D5) can be tested; τ-E has one voice per name and no accent labels; BFCL Audio has 225 clones and no breakdown.

**Honest sizing of the residual.** It is real but it is no longer "the first silent-wrong ledger for voice tool calls"; it is "the first ledger that (i) executes and schema-checks, (ii) crosses three pipelines with telephone band and fixed-SNR babble on identical clips, (iii) keeps WER as a per-cell covariate, and (iv) prices two signal-level and two prompt-level levers against each other including their false-positive bill." Items 3–5 are the ones no paper touches at all; items 1, 2, 7 are ours by construction but a reviewer will call them "τ-Elicitation / BFCL Audio with a different bank" unless the lever comparison and the pipeline × band cross carry the headline. D3's most defensible novel half is "confidence gating recovers under half and read-back over 80%, and here is what each costs on correct calls"; the read-back number alone is a replication of τ-E in a cascade.

**Reframed headline options (one sentence each, leading with the uncovered part).**

A. **"Same clip, three ears: where spoken-entity errors go silent."** On identical audio through Whisper-cascade, audio-LLM-transcriber and audio-native tool calling, across clean / 10 dB babble / 8 kHz mu-law / both, we report the executed silent-wrong rate by entity type with the asked-vs-silent split, and show that per-cell WER predicts it poorly once the band is narrowed, quantifying what BFCL Audio observed as failure shares and τ-Elicitation could not measure without an ASR.

B. **"Read back or gate? The false-positive bill for catching silent-wrong voice tool calls."** In cascades, where a confidence signal exists, we price read-back confirmation, ASR-confidence/n-best gating and an ask-when-unsure instruction against each other on recovery fraction, extra turns, tokens, and confirmations spent on calls that were already right, extending τ-Elicitation's audio-native scaffold result to the pipeline most deployments run and adding the lever it could not test.

C. **"The cascade asks, the native model executes."** Testing D2 directly: on matched clips, the audio-native pipeline raises fewer clarifications and a higher silent-wrong rate than the cascade under noise and telephone band, turning BFCL Audio's qualitative FM4/FM6 contrast into a per-entity rate with a WER covariate and a costed remedy.

Recommended: lead with A as the ledger paper and fold B in as the second half (levers with the false-positive bill); C becomes the headline finding inside A if D2 holds, and a reported reversal if it does not. Drop "and what read-back confirmation recovers" from the title; τ-Elicitation owns that phrase.

**Adjacent papers surfaced during the search, abstracts only, not full-text read here:** Audio2Tool (arXiv 2604.22821, ~30K queries, SpeechLMs and ASR-LLM pipelines under noise, smart-car/home/wearables) and Spoken Function Calling / SFC-Bench (arXiv 2608.05126, SLU-derived spoken functions for LALMs). Neither abstract mentions confirmation, clarification, silent errors or WER decoupling; both belong in related work as benchmarks. The coordinator should decide whether Audio2Tool merits a full read for its cascade-vs-SpeechLM-under-noise numbers.

## OVERALL VERDICT: GO-WITH-REFRAME

No single paper meets the MISSION stop condition. τ-Elicitation pre-empts the read-back-vs-instruction recovery number and the asked/silent calibration for audio-native elicitation agents; BFCL Audio pre-empts the two-suite cascade-vs-native benchmark and names the "valid but incorrect, never asks" cell without rating it. What no paper does: execute-and-schema-check the silent-wrong rate by entity type on identical clips across three pipelines, cross it with telephone band and fixed-SNR babble, keep WER as a per-cell covariate, and price confidence gating against read-back with the false-positive bill. Proceed only with the headline moved onto those four elements, D1 re-pre-registered against the published "numbers are easy" ordering, and both τ-Elicitation and BFCL Audio delineated in the abstract.
