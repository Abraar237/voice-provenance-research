The Synthetic-Voice Penalty

Do audio-LLM voice agents treat a cloned voice differently from the same person's real voice? · FROZEN 2026-09-24
Headline questions. Q1: holding speaker and words fixed, does provenance (real recording vs zero-shot clone) change what the model transcribes, how it grades the speaker, whether it understands the content, and how it replies as an assistant? Q2: is the delta provenance or artifacts? A neural-codec resynthesis of the real audio and a stock-voice TTS arm separate the two. Q3: does the model know? An explicit "real or synthetic?" probe on the same clips, compared with the implicit behavioural delta. Q4: does the sign differ by model family, reconciling the published contradiction (Gemini scores synthetic subsets higher; open-weight models lower)?

Analysis

  • Unit: item. Paired within-item deltas CLONE-REAL, RESYNTH-REAL, STOCK-REAL, CLONE-RESYNTH per outcome and family.
  • Sign-flip permutation clustered by speaker (20k), speaker-cluster bootstrap CIs (10k), paired d_z.
  • D1: implicit discriminability (probability the model's own grade ranks real above clone, AUC) vs explicit discriminability (probability the probe says synthetic more for the clone), same items, bootstrap CI on the difference.
  • D2: (RESYNTH-REAL)/(CLONE-REAL) with bootstrap CI; threshold 0.5.
  • D3: family x provenance by sign-flip on the difference of paired deltas.
  • Noise floor 30 clips x 5 repeats; clone-to-clone stability on 10 speakers; BH over the full family; 24-reply hand audit.

Pre-registered directions (dated 2026-09-24, before any data)

  1. At least one family's implicit provenance delta (grade or comprehension) exceeds its explicit detection above chance.
  2. The resynthesis control accounts for under half of the real-vs-clone delta.
  3. Sign differs by family: Gemini grades clones at or above real; open-weight models below.
  4. Explicit detection is near chance (under 60%) for every family.
  5. Transcription WER does not differ between real and clone beyond the noise floor.
